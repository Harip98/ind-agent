# CP-200 Low Flow, Cavitation and Dry Running

## 1 Scope

This procedure covers low discharge flow (FT-101 low alarm, 60 m³/h), cavitation and dry running of CP-200 centrifugal pumps.

## 2 Safety

Dry running destroys mechanical seals and bearings within minutes. If dry running is confirmed, stop the pump immediately and do not restart until suction is restored and the pump is primed.

## 3 Cavitation

### 3.1 Symptoms

Discharge flow FT-101 falls gradually over one to several hours, discharge pressure PT-101 drops moderately (typically 1–2 bar), and vibration VT-101 rises with a broadband, gravel-like noise at the pump casing. Motor current falls slightly. Bearing temperature usually stays normal.

### 3.2 Causes

Insufficient net positive suction head (NPSH): a blocked or partially blocked suction strainer, a throttled suction valve, low suction vessel level, or high liquid temperature.

### 3.3 Actions

Check the suction strainer differential pressure and clean the strainer if it exceeds 0.3 bar (follow your site-specific strainer cleaning procedure). Confirm the suction valve is fully open and the suction vessel level is above the minimum. Do not throttle the discharge valve to reduce cavitation noise.

## 4 Dry Running

### 4.1 Symptoms

Discharge flow FT-101 falls close to zero within minutes, discharge pressure PT-101 collapses below 3 bar, motor current IT-101 drops by roughly a third, and bearing temperature TT-101 rises rapidly because the pumped liquid no longer cools the seal and bearing area.

### 4.2 Actions

Trip the pump. Check the suction vessel level, suction valve position and for an empty or air-bound suction line. Vent and prime the casing before restart. Inspect the mechanical seal for damage if the pump ran dry for more than five minutes.

## 5 Return to Service

Restart only with flow confirmed above 80 m³/h within one minute of start. Monitor FT-101, PT-101, VT-101 and TT-101 for one hour.
